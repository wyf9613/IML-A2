\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{RQ1.}
Used car prices can be predicted effectively using the available structured vehicle features.
The best-performing model was Random Forest, with a test RMSE of 1793.69, MAE of 1205.22, and $R^2$ of 0.9570.
This means that it explained around 95.7\% of the price variance in the held-out test set.

\paragraph{RQ2.}
The model comparison shows that non-linear models performed better than the linear baseline, although their generalisation ability varied across model types.
Random Forest achieved the best test performance, with an RMSE of 1793.69 and $R^2$ of 0.9570, reducing test RMSE by 25.6\% compared with Ridge Regression.
KNN was the second-best model on the test set, but its much lower train RMSE of 201.33 compared with its test RMSE of 1924.35 suggests high variance and overfitting.
MLP also performed reasonably well, but it did not outperform Random Forest.
The single Decision Tree was weaker than the ensemble model, indicating that bagging helped improve both stability and accuracy.

\paragraph{RQ3.}
Preprocessing had a clear impact, especially for models that are sensitive to feature scale.
Standard scaling reduced Ridge RMSE from 7597.09 to 2444.76, and KNN RMSE from 6889.90 to 2097.92.
This shows that large differences in the ranges of mileage, tax, and engine size can affect linear regularisation and distance-based calculations.
Missing-value imputation helped retain usable training samples, while one-hot encoding allowed categorical variables such as brand, model, fuel type, and transmission to provide useful predictive information.
The log-price transformation also reduced target skewness from 2.29 to $-$0.13, making the regression task more stable.

\paragraph{RQ4.}
The most important predictor was \texttt{transmission\_Manual}, with a Random Forest importance score of 0.3347.
This was followed by \texttt{year} at 0.2143, \texttt{engineSize} at 0.1653, and \texttt{car\_age} at 0.0910.
These features reflect differences in vehicle specification, depreciation, and market segment.
In general, newer cars and cars with larger engines tend to have higher prices, while transmission type and luxury-brand status capture differences in vehicle class and buyer demand.

Overall, Random Forest is the best choice for prediction accuracy in this experiment, while future improvement should focus on richer vehicle-level data, gradient boosting models, and stronger interpretability checks.
